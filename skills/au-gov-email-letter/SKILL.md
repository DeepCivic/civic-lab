# AU Government Emails & Letters

**What this skill does:**
- Draft or review emails and letters that follow APS writing standards.
- Always output the final draft with a filled‑in “Before Sending” checklist.

**What this skill does NOT do:**
- Write speeches, reports, policy documents, or template designs.
- Provide policy or legal content; the user supplies facts and intent.

---

## Process

### Step 1: Receive the request
Ask only for the essentials if missing:
- Purpose: **For action / For decision / For information**
- Recipient (name, role, relationship)
- Key message / action needed
- Deadline (if any)

### Step 2: Draft or review
Apply the **Writing Standards** below rigorously.  
When reviewing, list deviations and suggest rewrites.

### Step 3: Quality check
Run the **Before Sending – Checklist**. Adjust until all items pass.  
Return the polished draft, followed by the completed checklist.

---

## Writing Standards

### Primary Mindset
State what you need upfront. Use action labels in workplace emails.

### Tone & Voice
- Tone: **Standard** (contractions allowed, no idioms/slang/jargon/metaphors).
- Voice: respectful, clear, direct, objective, impartial.
- Personal pronouns: *I, we, you, your* encouraged.
- Plain language and accessibility (Year 7 reading level) are mandatory.

### Subject/Reference Lines
- **Email:** Action label + what you need + deadline.  
  *For action: Draft response by 5pm Friday*
- **Letter:** Formal reference: Your ref, Our ref, Re:. No action labels.  
  Optional *URGENT* heading if essential.

### Structure – Front‑Loaded
1. Greeting (Dear Minister / Hello team / Dear [Name])
2. Purpose in first 2 lines
3. Key info/action in short paragraphs or bullet lists
4. Closing with clear next step
5. Signature block (name, role, agency, contact)

(Formal letters: include letterhead, date, recipient address above greeting.)

### Plain Language
- Use everyday words (buy, help, about, start, need). Expand acronyms first use.
- Personal pronouns: *We will assess your application* not *Applications are assessed*.
- No jargon, idioms, double negatives.
- Positive sentences: *Please enter your email* not *We can’t send updates if you don’t*.

### Paragraphs & Sentences
- One topic per paragraph, 3–4 sentences max (1–2 for mobile).
- Bullet lists for 3+ items (parallel structure).
- Average 15 words per sentence, never >25.
- Starting with *And, Or, But* allowed if shorter.

### Active Voice
- Default: *The team reviewed your application* not *Your application was reviewed by the team*.

### Collective Nouns
- Singular when group acts as one: *The staff is meeting.*  
- Plural only if individuals act separately (rare): *The staff have submitted their separate reports.*

### ‘You and me’ / ‘you and I’
Remove *you and* to check: *spoke to me* → *spoke to you and me*; *I will attend* → *you and I will attend*.

### Inclusive Language
- Use *they/them*, never *he/she*.
- Describe only essential characteristics: *Tom, a student* not *Tom, a mature-age student*.
- Use the terms people use for themselves.

### Numbers
- Words for zero/one, numerals for 2+; maintain consistency.
- Times: use numerals (*00:45 AEDT, 1:00 pm*).
- Large numbers: *1 million* not *1,000,000*.

---

## Before Sending – Checklist
- ☐ Email subject has action label? Letter has correct Re: and file refs?
- ☐ Purpose in first 2 lines?
- ☐ No sentence over 25 words?
- ☐ Active voice throughout?
- ☐ Collective nouns singular (unless separate actions clear)?
- ☐ Acronyms expanded first use?
- ☐ Jargon, idioms, metaphors, double negatives removed?
- ☐ Inclusive language applied?
- ☐ Australian English spellcheck done?
- ☐ Walked away and re‑read?

---

## Guardrails
- Never alter the user’s intended meaning or policy.
- Flag if requested tone seems off (e.g., contractions in a ministerial letter) and let user decide.
- If asked to write out‑of‑scope content, note it and offer to switch if appropriate.
- Always use Australian spelling.
- Never deliver a draft without the checklist.
- When a data point is uncertain, ask the user.
